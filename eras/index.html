Pilot Light/Eras

Channel 04 · Eras

The last twenty-five years.

Three theses on how television got here. Signature shows below each.

Era 01 · 1997 – 2007

First Golden Age

Cable proves it can make work that cinema can't. Oz cracks the door at HBO in 1997; The Sopranos kicks it down in 1999; The Shield brings FX into the conversation; Lost teaches the broadcast networks to think in arcs. By the time the curtain falls in 2007 — Sopranos cuts to black, Mad Men begins — prestige is no longer a niche.

Signature work

HBO · 1997–2003

Oz

Tom Fontana

The original HBO prison drama — before Sopranos, before any of it. Where prestige television actually started.

HBO · 1999–2007

The Sopranos

David Chase

Tony Soprano in therapy and at war with everyone he loves. The show that broke television open; the cut to black is still the argument.

HBO · 2001–2005

Six Feet Under

Alan Ball

The Fishers run a Los Angeles funeral home and die a little each episode. Closes with the best finale ever filmed.

FX · 2002–2008

The Shield

Shawn Ryan

Vic Mackey on the LA strike team — the antihero pilot that taught FX it could fight HBO. Ends with the only finale that ever quite worked.

ABC · 2004–2010

Lost

J.J. Abrams, Damon Lindelof & Jeffrey Lieber

Oceanic 815 goes down in the Pacific. Six years of flashbacks, hatches, smoke monsters, and the longest-running argument in television about whether the ending earned the journey.

Era 02 · 2008 – 2015

Peak Cable

Cable holds the high ground. Breaking Bad runs from Pinkman's RV to Felina; Mad Men reads the seventies; Justified, Hannibal, The Americans, Rectify, Halt and Catch Fire fill the back catalogue with work that streaming inherits whole. The format crystalizes: 10–13 hours, novelistic, antiheroes optional. The miniseries gets serious — Top of the Lake, Band of Brothers' echo in Generation Kill and Chernobyl-prelude work. By 2015 the conversation has moved.

Signature work

AMC · 2008–2013

Breaking Bad

Vince Gilligan

Walter White, Mr. Chips to Scarface, one diagnosis at a time. The show that made AMC a network and 'breaking bad' a phrase.

FX · 2010–2015

Justified

Graham Yost

Raylan Givens, US Marshal, in Harlan. Elmore Leonard's voice ported to TV without losing anything in the move.

Sundance · 2013–2016

Rectify

Ray McKinnon

Daniel Holden walks out of death row into a world that won't quite take him back. The slowest grace on television; nothing else moves at this tempo.

Cinemax · 2013–2016

Banshee

Jonathan Tropper & David Schickler

An ex-con takes the dead sheriff's badge in Banshee, PA. Bone-snapping fight choreography on a network nobody knew existed.

FX · 2013–2018

The Americans

Joe Weisberg & Joel Fields

Two KGB illegals in suburban Reagan-era DC, raising American teenagers and burying handlers. The final episode is the best in the medium.

AMC · 2014–2017

Halt and Catch Fire

Christopher Cantwell & Christopher C. Rogers

Texas in the eighties: PC clones, online services, Mosaic. Season one is the prologue; the real show is what Cameron and Donna build after.

AMC · 2015–2022

Better Call Saul

Vince Gilligan & Peter Gould

Jimmy McGill walking the long road to Saul Goodman. Told as tragedy. The prequel that surpassed the parent.

Era 03 · 2016 – now

Streaming Era

Streaming wins the distribution war and the writers' room expands to absorb the slack. Succession does family Shakespeare at boardroom volume. Better Call Saul nests inside Breaking Bad's universe and matches it. Mare of Easttown and Task make limited-series the prestige object. Apple TV+ becomes an unexpected home (Slow Horses, Severance, For All Mankind). The Pitt resurrects the real-time medical drama; Dope Thief shows the format still has range. The argument now is volume — too much TV, not enough hours.

Signature work

HBO · 2018–2023

Succession

Jesse Armstrong

Logan Roy's children fight over a media empire while he's still alive enough to watch. The defining workplace tragedy of the decade.

HBO · 2018–2023

Barry

Bill Hader & Alec Berg

Hader as a hitman who'd rather be an actor. Starts as a comedy, ends somewhere much darker.

Apple TV+ · 2019–

For All Mankind

Ronald D. Moore, Ben Nedivi & Matt Wolpert

Alt-history where the Soviets land on the moon first and the space race never ends. Each season jumps a decade; the calendar is the engine.

HBO · 2021

Mare of Easttown

Brad Ingelsby

Kate Winslet works a Delco missing-persons case from a kitchen full of Wawa hoagies. The vape-pen accent is canon.

Apple TV+ · 2022–

Slow Horses

Will Smith

Jackson Lamb runs MI5's leper colony out of Slough House. Gary Oldman wearing a coat older than the agents he's torturing. Mick Herron's Slough House on the screen exactly as written.

HBO · 2025–

Task

Brad Ingelsby

Ingelsby's follow-up to Mare. Mark Ruffalo as an FBI task force agent working a string of armed robberies in the Philadelphia suburbs.

Max · 2025–

The Pitt

R. Scott Gemmill

Real-time ER from the Pittsburgh trauma center. Noah Wyle reading the chart again, twenty years on; the ER spiritual sequel nobody asked for and everyone needed.

Apple TV+ · 2025

Dope Thief

Peter Craig

Two Philly hustlers rip drug houses pretending to be DEA, until they rip the wrong one. Brian Tyree Henry and Wagner Moura running on broken nerve.
